**“Sharp Quantitative Stability of Finite Projective Records under Approximately Commuting Unitary Transformations”**

by Bertrand Dalimier (Independent Researcher) for publication as a regular research article in *Linear Algebra and its Applications*.

The paper studies a finite family of mutually orthogonal projections \((P_i)_{i\in I}\) summing to the identity and a unitary operator \(U\). It compares the quadratic cellwise commutator budget
\[
\delta^2=\sum_i \|[P_i,U]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\]
with the largest aggregate-cut commutator
\[
C_{\max}=\max_{S\subseteq I}\left\|\left[\sum_{i\in S}P_i,U\right]\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]

The main contributions are fourfold. First, the paper proves the sharp finite-dimensional inequality \(C_{\max}^2\le \delta^2/2\). Second, for positive defect it characterizes equality exactly: saturation occurs if and only if precisely two cells have nonzero commutator defect. Third, it proves a dimension-free inverse stability theorem showing that near saturation forces quantitative concentration of the quadratic defect on two cells, with the explicit modulus
\[
2\eta+\frac{36\eta}{(1-3\eta)\rho^2}
\]
at defect scale \(\delta\ge\rho>0\). Fourth, an explicit three-cell family proves that the inverse-square dependence on the defect scale is necessary up to multiplicative constants; the corresponding scaled asymptotic ratio tends to \(4\).

Equivalently, for two finite projective measurements with matching rank profiles, the cut envelope is their operational distance and the main estimate becomes
\[
D_{\mathrm{op}}(P,Q)^2\le \frac12\sum_i\|P_i-Q_i\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2.
\]
The manuscript develops the equality and near-equality structure of this bound.

The manuscript is positioned as a finite-dimensional matrix/operator result rather than as an approximation-to-a-commuting-tuple theorem. The projections and the unitary are kept fixed; the inverse result concerns the internal structure of the existing commutator profile in the near-equality regime.

A companion Lean 4 development formally verifies the principal inequality, equality classification, quantitative modulus, rank-one formula, explicit sharpness family, and asymptotic obstruction. The manuscript describes this formalization as a reproducibility and verification artifact; the mathematical contribution is the operator-theoretic result itself.
